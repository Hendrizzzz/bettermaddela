**Research date:** 2026 August 26
**Prepared for:** BetterMaddela visual storytelling timeline (research input only — NOT production data)
**Method:** Web fetching of statutes, government issuances, encyclopedic entries, and local histories. Every claim below is tagged by evidence tier:

- **[A] Documented legal fact** — full statutory/executive text retrieved directly from a primary or official-journal source (lawphil.net, Official Gazette).
- **[B] Secondary/local history** — published narrative histories (blogs, encyclopedia articles, Wikipedia citing local compilers). Useful for storytelling, but not independently corroborated against archives.

## 1. Earliest inhabitants: Bugkalot (Ilongot) and other indigenous peoples

### What the sources actually say

**[B] The dominant local-history account** ("HISTORICAL BACKGROUND OF MADDELA," compiled by Glory S. Almoite et al., posted on the *I Love Quirino* blog, Sept 4, 2013 — this is the source Wikipedia's Maddela History section cites):

> "Before the advent of migration and long before the approach of development planning for urban settlement or resettlement, Maddela was inhabited by natives who lived among the fingers of the Sierra Madre Mountains and in the hinterlands of the Mamparang Ranges. The natives were the Ilongots (or Bugcalots) and the Dumagats… Of the two tribes, the Ilongots showed more sophistication. They occupied the western upland portion of the territory and also upstream of the Cagayan River up to Dupax, Nueva Vizcaya. They lived in villages with organized but simple governments headed by a director of non-Christian. The Dumagats occupied the eastern portion of Cagayan River, and along the Ngilinan and Manglad streams…"

The same account says the incoming Ilocano settlers "forged friendly relation[s] with the natives who, as the years drooled [sic], learned the basics of agriculture," including intermarriages. **Note:** this framing ("friendly relations," natives "learning" agriculture) is a settler-authored narrative; it does not describe displacement explicitly, even though displacement is its implicit backdrop. Treat the interpretation, not just the facts, critically.

**[B] Province-wide context** (English Wikipedia, *Quirino*, History → Early history, citing Pedro Salgado, *Cagayan Valley and Eastern Cordillera: 1581–1898, Vol. II*, Rex Publishing, 2002):

> "Quirino was the forest region of the province of Nueva Vizcaya, inhabited by tribal groups known as the Negritos… Aside from the Negritos, the area was also inhabited by Ilongot people, who were feared for their headhunting raids… Throughout the period of Spanish colonization, the province was one of the few remaining unconquered areas in the Philippines due to its remoteness, having only seen a Spanish military expedition in 1848 and the brief presence of Spanish missionaries in 1891."

Salgado (2002) is a published regional historian; this is a reasonable secondary-academic anchor, but I could not retrieve the book itself — verify page numbers before quoting in print.

**[B] Ethnographic background** (*Worldmark Encyclopedia of Cultures and Daily Life*, via Encyclopedia.com, "Ilongot"):
- The Ilongot self-designate as "Bugkalut"; the lowlander name "Ilongot" derives from 'irungut, "forest people."
- Homeland described as rolling hills at the junction of the Caraballo Range and Sierra Madre, drained by the headwaters of the Cagayan — i.e., exactly the Maddela/Nagtipunan/Dupax borderlands.
- "Although Spanish soldiers and missionaries had penetrated the upper Cagayan valley by the beginning of the 17th century, they had little impact on the Ilongot whose reputation as a fierce, wild, and unsubjugated people endured well into the American period."
- On displacement: "The arrival in recent decades of the lumbering industry and the airstrips of the New Tribes Mission (Protestant) have ended Ilongot isolation… farmers of Christian ethnic groups are encroaching inexorably upon Ilongot territory. Violent confrontations between Ilongot and settlers have been frequent and even reached the notice of the national press in the 1960s."

**[B] Modern population** (English Wikipedia, *Bugkalot*, citing Philippine Statistics Authority, Ethnicity in the Philippines, 2020 Census): 18,712 Bugkalot nationally (2020 census); provinces listed include Nueva Vizcaya, Nueva Ecija, Quirino, and Aurora. Key anthropological references cited there: Renato Rosaldo, *Ilongot Headhunting, 1883–1974: A Study in Society and History* (Stanford University Press, 1980), and Henrik Hvenegaard Mikkelsen, "Chaosmology: Shamanism and Personhood Among the Bugkalot," *HAU: Journal of Ethnographic Theory* 6(1), 2016.

### ⚠️ Conflict flag
The Encyclopedia.com entry gives "50,786 (1990)" population while the 2020 PSA figure is 18,712. These are almost certainly incompatible methodologies/censuses (Worldmark figures are considered unreliable for Philippine ethnic groups). Do not use either number on the timeline without choosing one clearly sourced figure.

### Assessment
The presence of Bugkalot/Ilongot communities in the Maddela area before mass migration is supported by multiple independent secondary sources ([B]) and is consistent with academic ethnography, but **no source retrieved here documents a specific displacement event in Maddela itself with dates**. For the timeline, present indigenous presence as established, and displacement/settler encroachment as a documented regional process, without inventing local specifics.

## 2. Etymology and early names: "Pinappagan"

### What is documented

**[A] "Pinappagan" existed as an official place name.** Executive Order No. 368, s. 1950 (Official Gazette, signed November 11, 1950) states:

> "The municipal districts of Imugan, Kasibu, **Pinappagan**, Pingkian, and Kayapa are hereby abolished."

and simultaneously organizes the new **Municipality of Maddela** out of that territory. So the renaming sequence (Pinappagan → Maddela, 1950) is confirmed by the primary document itself, independent of any blog.

**[B]** Peoplaid ("Maddela Profile," Mar 21, 2020, updated Apr 22, 2023): "Maddela was formerly called Pinappagan which was formerly a territory of Nueva Vizcaya Province… It was named after former Nueva Vizcaya Governor Tomas Maddela."

**[B]** English Wikipedia Maddela infobox: `other_name = Pinappagan`, `named_for = Tomas Maddela`.

### Origin stories / meaning of the name

**Not found.** None of the sources retrieved gives a linguistic meaning or folk-origin story for "Pinappagan" (e.g., a root word, a legend about the valley being a pasture/ranch ground, etc.). The Almoite et al. account uses it only as a geographic label ("the valley of Pinappagan, now Maddela"). Any etymological story circulating locally is currently **[C] unverifiable** for this project.

The claim that the town was named for **Nueva Vizcaya Governor Tomás Maddela** appears in both Peoplaid and Wikipedia but neither cites a contemporaneous document. It is plausibly correct (it would be typical practice in 1950, under a Quirino-era reorganization of Nueva Vizcaya) but should be labeled **[B]/[C] — widely repeated, not yet document-verified**.

## 3. When did organized settlement begin?

### The local-history narrative ([B], Almoite et al. 2013)

- **1919:** "a group of 20 Ilocanos headed by Forester Vicente Velasco arrived and put up settlements along the Cagayan River. Gradually, they moved into the valley of Pinappagan, now Maddela." Crops planted: palay, tobacco, corn, peanut.
- **1922–1925:** start of permanent settlement; military government headed by **Lt. Quintin Alcantara**, who opened a road connecting Pinappagan and Panang.
- **~Three years later (≈1925–1928):** "Pinappagan was made a municipal District attached to the town of Bagabag, Nueva Vizcaya. Then Nueva Vizcaya Governor Juan Manzano appointed **Eusebio Martin** as first President of the District…" Peoplaid puts this at **1928** explicitly.
- **First barrios of the municipal district:** "Villa Hermosa, **Dumabato**, Sto. Niño, Dipintin, Abbag and San Pedro."
- District presidents succeeding Martin: Rafael Daguio, Federico Ramos, Rodrigo Pascual (to WWII); Japanese occupation: Marcos Pimentel and Fernando Castillo alternately as district mayors.
- **1950:** renamed Maddela, regular town, first mayor **Jose Ancheta** (re-elected, died in office; vice mayor Gregorio Baroma served 1953–1955). First regular local election **1956**: Dionisio Sarandi elected mayor; many new barrios created during his terms.

### Dumabato specifically
Dumabato appears **only** in the list of first barrios above ([B]). No retrieved source elaborates on Dumabato's age, its role in the American-era Ilongot administration, or whether it predates the 20th century. Historical literature on the Ilongot often mentions riverine posts/reservations in this general region, but I could not verify a Dumabato-specific claim. **Treat any pre-1919 Dumabato story as [C] until verified.**

### Spanish-era prehistory ([B], Salgado via Wikipedia-*Quirino*)
Spanish military expedition in 1848; brief missionary presence in 1891; otherwise effectively unconquered interior. No permanent Spanish settlement in the Maddela area is claimed by any retrieved source.

### "Ilongot reservation era"
**Could not be verified from retrieved sources.** The existence of American-era Ilongot reservations in the wider region is discussed in academic literature (Rosaldo; American-era reports), but nothing retrieved ties a specific reservation to the Maddela/Pinappagan area with dates and legal basis. Listed under Evidence Gaps.

### Answer to "was Maddela created earlier?"
Yes — as a **municipal district** (attached to Bagabag, Nueva Vizcaya, ca. mid/late 1920s per [B] accounts), and as a full **municipality by EO 368 on November 11, 1950**. No statute or executive order creating a municipality of "Maddela" earlier than 1950 was found.

### ⚠️ Conflict flag — EO number
Peoplaid states Maddela was created "by virtue of **Executive Order No. 364** signed by President Elpidio Quirino" on November 11, 1950. This contradicts the Official Gazette (EO **368**) and Batas Pambansa 345 (which cites "Executive Order No. 368 dated November 11, 1950"). **Resolved in favor of EO 368** — two primary/independent confirmations vs. one tertiary page. Do not use "EO 364."

## 5. Notable transformations

### Migration ([B])
Ilocano pioneer settlement from 1919 (Velasco group), expanding through the 1920s; later commerce with San Agustin and Jones, Isabela. Wikipedia-*Quirino* notes the province was named for President Elpidio Quirino partly because of the Ilocano settlers in the area. Tagalog and other Christian-settler migration is asserted generally (e.g., Encyclopedia.com lists "Christian Gaddang, Isinai, Tagalog, and Ilocanos have settled the surrounding lowlands") but no Maddela-specific Tagalog migration dates were retrieved.

### Logging era ([C]→[B])
No Maddela-specific logging history was retrieved. Regionally, Encyclopedia.com notes "the arrival in recent decades of the lumbering industry… have ended Ilongot isolation," and Wikipedia-*Quirino* links the area to "Deforestation in the Philippines during the Marcos administration." A Maddela-specific logging timeline (concession names, log pond era, ban dates) is **unverified** — see Gaps.

### Hydropower — Casecnan ([A] for project facts; [C] for Maddela-local impacts)
Casecnan Irrigation and Hydroelectric Plant diverts water from the **Casecnan and Taan rivers** (headwaters in the Quirino highlands, formerly part of greater Maddela / now Nagtipunan side) through a 26-km tunnel to the Pantabangan Reservoir, Nueva Ecija. Watershed Forest Reserve established by **EO 136 (Aug 1987)** covering 57,930 ha; construction approved 1995 under the Ramos flagship program; tunnel opened September 2001; plant commissioned **2002**; ~140 MW operational capacity (150 MW planned); owners NPC/NIA. Sources: Wikipedia-*Casecnan Dam* (with ECA-Watch field report and DOT citations). **Important caveat:** the dam structure itself is in Rizal, Nueva Ecija — do not depict it as located in Maddela. Impacts on Bugkalot communities in the Quirino headwaters are reported in advocacy literature but were not verified here.

### Roads — Maharlika Highway ([C])
It is common knowledge that Maddela lies on the Pan-Philippine Highway/Maharlika corridor through southern Quirino, but **no retrieved source explicitly states this**, and the AH26/Pan-Philippine Highway check returned only a redirect without Maddela-level detail. Date of highway construction through Maddela (often linked to post-war national road programs) is **unverified**. Needs DPWH records or local sources before appearing on the timeline.
